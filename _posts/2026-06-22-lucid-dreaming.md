# Lucid Dreaming / Consciousness
I woke before my alarm and laid in my bed. This is a normal occurence, but something felt different today. I was grinding my teeth and my mouth and throat were incredibly dry. I was uniquely aware of how comfortable I was laying in my warm bed next to my loving wife. Even though my mouth was drier than the Sahara I felt paralyzed; all I had to do was get up out of bed and take a sip from the tap, but I was rooted to the spot. Each time I swallowed I could feel the entirety of my esophagus contracting, each segment sticking to itself all the way down to my stomach. Simultaneously I was grinding my teeth as my brain swirled in the aether. My eyesight was consumed by visions of the void; the fractal landscape affected not only my mind but also my physical reality. As I pitched and yawed around the different lobes of my fractal universe my perception of time ebbed and flowed; I pitched and yawed within the fractal void; my muscles contracted and relaxed; the perception of the world around be drifted between my own and the perspective of the conscious collective; I was drifting somewhere between a wakeful state and sleep. As I continued laying in this state I contemplated my consumption of alcohol. I was golfing in a couple of days and simply didn't want to drink, but felt pressured. Somewhere along the line, the decision was made: I was not going to drink during this golf outing. This waffling between states experience continued until the superposition collapsed (my alarm went off). I got up out of bed and went about my day as I do any other day.

This is not the first time I've had an experience like this, though it is the most traditionally sober I've been during one of them. The first few times I had most certainly been under the influence of hallucinogens such as LSD and psylocibin mushrooms. One experience in college shook me to the point of hysterical crying, after which I called my mom and spent the weekend at her house. During that experience the visualization of the void (everything, and nothing; the trajectories of my life which I would both experience and not experience) had become clear to me in the form of infinity. If the two dimensional infinity was rendered in three dimensions, our view of it from the side would be a line. As it rotated, though, I could see my own realms and realities within it. Both descending into the symbol itself as well as extending out of the symbol. Each reality was formed from the division of the reality before it. Each time I had made a decision, there existed another, parallel reality where I had performed the other available action. These universes split, spiralled, and perception of those further away realities faded from my perception. In this way, I was one with all of the different versions of me; which, as the realities diverged, could not be called me at all. This was my own collective conscious. It was still my own story, but within that story I had made all of the decisions which someone much different than me would have made. After the core essence of this "ego death" faded, I attempted to manifest the one single thing I needed in my life: love. There was a specific person I had in mind at the time, but the only real thing on my mind was love. Unconditional, unending, total love was the core of my meditation. I gazed out of the window of my college dorm/apartment on a grey and cloudy sky. As the sun rose and time broke the trance, I went into the woods and cried harder than I had in years. I called one of my friends and he came and consoled me, though he had to leave. After that I called my mom, armed with my new understanding of the way the world worked. I ended up staying for the weekend at her house.

After this experience I saw an intricate weave of myself within the realities that other people were living. Sometimes I could never understand how a person would make a decision that me, myself in this reality would never make. On reflection I realize that I don't need to realize why they would make that decision; all that mattered was acceptance that they made that choice, respecting the choice they made, integrating that decision into my own reality's bounds, and (if necessary) forgiveness for making that decision. Forgiveness came easier, because infinity had shown me that I had made that decision once as well; all I was forgiving was myself.

After overcoming my fear of hallucinogens that came on after my initial jarring experience, I had a similar, though somewhat muted, experience as I tripped acid again. On every trip, whether it was mushrooms or LSD, I always hated talking. I could see the most vivid hallucinations, hear the sweetest music, or reckon with the deepest meaning of the universe within my internal dialogue. The second I opened my mouth, the subject of my speech was ruined. These experiences were not meant to be shared in such a way. So I attempted to draw what I was feeling, thinking, and seeing. I ended up creating some of my favorite pieces of art I've created to date. Simply through channeling my experiences through pen and paper. I was really pleased at how well this worked. It was calming to finally have the means to express what I was really going through internally without fouling the divine. As I was listening to music afterwards, another portion of the fractal was revealed to me. The ascending and descending units were simply stairs. Steps, decisions, choices, actions to be taken which would either go up (good, healthy, constructive choices) or down (bad, harmful, destructive choices). After some reflection I came to the staircase representing my relationship with my then girlfriend (now my wife). I realized that all I had to do was take the next step upward. I realized that I really did love this woman, and I wanted to be with her. In order to do that I had to keep stepping up. Not only did I have to take the step, but I also had to carve the staircase. I saw that it wasn't enough to simply wait until a step was presented to me to take it. I had to be proactive in finding them. I saw several ways to do this, including (but not limited to):
    - Anticipating needs; not just her needs or my needs, but what our relationship would need to grow and thrive.
    - Being honest with myself; I previously fell into the habit of telling myself the same thing over and over, eventually goading myself into believing it, even if it wasn't true.
    - Being able to change; some things about ourselves are truly cemented, but is challenging ourselves to be better really such a bad thing?

After several years of contemplation, this most recent event has given me the realization that the fractal isn't just me. I am just a lobe of this fractal universe; I am the universe having a human experience. Just as everyone else is the universe having a human experience. They are simply different lobes of the same fractal. Different parts of the same whole. This is probably the most direct comparison I can draw to God. I am a product of the Catholic school system, and in that school system we are taught that God is inside all of us. I actually think this is true, and I think that some of this insight might be me ceding that I agree with some of the Catholic teaching (the majority of which I do not agree with). It is actually bewildering that I, a staunch atheist, have had this change of heart/mind/soul. And yet, every time I get a glimpse of this inner fractal, this secret universe, I am left reeling at the beauty, chaos, and magnitude of what I have witnessed. I would also make the argument that this isn't God in the traditional Catholic sense either; I am not compelled to go to church every Sunday and recite ancient texts in honor of His glory.

What's the point I'm trying to make here? I suppose I'm just trying to document my experiences with higher levels of consciousness in hopes that someone else has had some similar experience and would like to expound upon that. I have much more to add, though I am impatient and eager to get this text out of my brain. This can probably be left as a living blog post which I can come back and edit as I like.
